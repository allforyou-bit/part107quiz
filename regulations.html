Part 107 Regulations Practice Test

Regulations make up 15–25% of the real exam and are the most memorization-friendly points on the test: the 400-ft ceiling, the 100-mph speed limit, the $500/10-day accident rule, bottle-to-throttle, and who's really in command. All 41 regulations questions, with answers and references

The drill above shuffles the answer choices on every attempt. Below is the same 41-question set written out in full, in a fixed order, so you can read it straight through, print it, or check a single rule without restarting a quiz. Each answer carries the reasoning and the FAA source it rests on.

  1. Under Part 107, a small unmanned aircraft must weigh less than how much at takeoff, including everything on board or attached to it?

    • A. 25 pounds
    • B. 55 pounds Correct answer
    • C. 75 pounds
    • D. 100 pounds, including any attached payload

    Why: Part 107 applies to small unmanned aircraft weighing less than 55 lb (25 kg) at takeoff, including all payload and anything attached.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.3

  2. What is the maximum altitude you may fly a small UA under Part 107, unless you stay within 400 feet of a structure?

    • A. 400 ft AGL Correct answer
    • B. 500 ft AGL
    • C. 400 ft MSL, regardless of terrain elevation
    • D. 1,200 ft AGL

    Why: The ceiling is 400 ft above ground level. You may go higher only within a 400-ft radius of a structure, and then no more than 400 ft above the structure's top.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.51(b)

  3. What is the maximum groundspeed allowed for a small unmanned aircraft under Part 107?

    • A. 87 mph
    • B. 100 mph (87 knots) Correct answer
    • C. 120 mph when operating in Class G airspace
    • D. 55 knots

    Why: The groundspeed limit is 100 mph, which equals 87 knots. Remember it is groundspeed, not airspeed, so a tailwind counts against you.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.51(a)

  4. What is the minimum age to hold a remote pilot certificate with an sUAS rating?

    • A. 18 years old
    • B. 14 years old
    • C. 16 years old Correct answer
    • D. 21 years old

    Why: You must be at least 16 years old to be eligible for a remote pilot certificate.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.61

  5. To keep exercising the privileges of a remote pilot certificate, how often must you complete recurrent training?

    • A. Every 12 calendar months
    • B. Every 36 calendar months
    • C. Only once, before the initial knowledge test
    • D. Every 24 calendar months Correct answer

    Why: Recurrent training (free, online) must be completed within the previous 24 calendar months to operate under Part 107. The certificate itself does not expire.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.65

  6. How soon must you report a qualifying sUAS accident to the FAA?

    • A. Immediately by phone
    • B. Within 24 hours
    • C. Within 10 calendar days Correct answer
    • D. Within 30 calendar days of the event

    Why: A report is required no later than 10 calendar days after an operation that causes serious injury, loss of consciousness, or qualifying property damage.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.9

  7. Which event requires an accident report to the FAA under Part 107?

    • A. Any damage to any property, no matter how small the cost
    • B. Serious injury, or at least $500 damage to property other than the drone Correct answer
    • C. Only an accident involving a fatality
    • D. Damage to the drone itself costing more than $500 to repair

    Why: Report when there is serious injury or loss of consciousness, or repair/replacement of damaged property OTHER than the UA costs $500 or more.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.9

  8. What is the minimum time that must pass between consuming alcohol and operating a small UA ('bottle to throttle')?

    • A. 12 hours
    • B. 4 hours
    • C. 24 hours
    • D. 8 hours Correct answer

    Why: No one may act as a crewmember within 8 hours of consuming alcohol, while under its influence, or with a blood alcohol concentration of .04 or greater.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.27, 91.17

  9. A person may not operate an sUAS with a blood alcohol concentration at or above what level?

    • A. 0.08
    • B. 0.02
    • C. 0.04 Correct answer
    • D. 0.10

    Why: The limit that applies to airmen, including remote pilots, is .04 BAC — stricter than the typical .08 driving limit.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.27, 91.17

  10. What does it cost to register a drone for Part 107 operations, and how long is the registration valid?

    • A. $5 per aircraft, valid 3 years Correct answer
    • B. $5 per pilot for all aircraft, valid 1 year
    • C. Free, valid forever
    • D. $25 per aircraft, valid 3 years

    Why: Part 107 registration in FAA DroneZone is $5 per aircraft and lasts 3 years. The registration number must be marked on the outside of the aircraft.

    Reference: 14 CFR Part 48

  11. Upon request, the remote pilot in command must present the remote pilot certificate to which of the following?

    • A. Only FAA aviation safety inspectors conducting a formal ramp inspection at the flight site
    • B. The FAA, NTSB, TSA, or any federal, state, or local law enforcement officer Correct answer
    • C. Airport managers within 5 NM
    • D. Insurance company adjusters

    Why: 107.7 requires presenting the certificate and identification on request to the FAA, NTSB, TSA, or law enforcement.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.7

  12. During an in-flight emergency, what deviation authority does the remote pilot in command have?

    • A. None — the rules always apply
    • B. May deviate from any Part 107 rule to the extent required to meet the emergency, and must send a written report if the FAA requests one Correct answer
    • C. May deviate only after getting ATC approval by radio
    • D. May deviate only below 200 ft AGL

    Why: In an emergency requiring immediate action, the RPIC may deviate from any rule of Part 107 to the extent needed. The FAA may request a written report afterward.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.21

  13. Without a waiver, how many small unmanned aircraft may one remote pilot operate at the same time?

    • A. One Correct answer
    • B. Two, with a visual observer
    • C. Three, if all are under 2 lb
    • D. As many as the pilot can safely watch

    Why: A person may manipulate the controls of, or act as RPIC for, only one UA at a time unless the FAA has granted a 107.35 waiver.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.35

  14. Under Part 107, carrying hazardous materials on a small unmanned aircraft is:

    • A. Allowed with proper placards
    • B. Allowed if the total weight stays under 55 lb
    • C. Prohibited Correct answer
    • D. Allowed only over unpopulated areas

    Why: 107.36 flatly prohibits the carriage of hazardous materials (as defined in 49 CFR 171.8), including spare lithium batteries carried as cargo.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.36

  15. A small unmanned aircraft must yield the right of way to:

    • A. All other aircraft Correct answer
    • B. Only manned aircraft in distress
    • C. Only aircraft below 400 ft AGL
    • D. Only other unmanned aircraft

    Why: The sUAS must yield to ALL aircraft, airborne vehicles, and launch/reentry vehicles, and may not pass over, under, or ahead of them unless well clear.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.37

  16. Operating a small UA from a moving land vehicle is permitted only:

    • A. Never, under any circumstances
    • B. Anywhere, as long as groundspeed stays under 100 mph
    • C. Over a sparsely populated area, and not transporting property for compensation Correct answer
    • D. At night with anti-collision lighting

    Why: 107.25 allows operating from a moving land or water vehicle only over sparsely populated areas, and never while transporting property for compensation or hire. Operating from a moving aircraft is always prohibited.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.25

  17. Dropping material from a small unmanned aircraft in flight is:

    • A. Always prohibited
    • B. Allowed only with a Part 107 waiver approved through the FAA DroneZone portal
    • C. Allowed only over your own property
    • D. Allowed if it creates no undue hazard to people or property Correct answer

    Why: 107.23 prohibits allowing an object to be dropped in a way that creates an undue hazard. A safe, controlled drop over a clear area is not itself illegal.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.23

  18. Before each flight, the remote pilot in command must:

    • A. Inspect the sUAS to ensure it is in a condition for safe operation Correct answer
    • B. File a flight plan with the FAA at least 24 hours before launch
    • C. Notify the nearest control tower
    • D. Log the flight in IACRA

    Why: 107.49 requires a preflight check of the aircraft, control links, power supply, and the operating environment before every flight.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.49

  19. A person who does NOT hold a remote pilot certificate may fly the controls of an sUAS only if:

    • A. They are at least 16 years old
    • B. They have completed the TRUST test
    • C. A visual observer is watching the aircraft
    • D. A remote pilot in command directly supervises them and can take immediate control Correct answer

    Why: Non-certificated persons may manipulate the controls only under the direct supervision of an RPIC who is able to take immediate control of the aircraft.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.12, 107.19

  20. Who is directly responsible for, and the final authority on, the operation of a small unmanned aircraft system?

    • A. The drone's registered owner
    • B. The visual observer
    • C. The remote pilot in command Correct answer
    • D. The client who hired the flight

    Why: The RPIC is directly responsible for and is the final authority as to the operation of the sUAS.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.19

  21. How do you obtain authorization to operate in controlled airspace under Part 107?

    • A. From the airport manager
    • B. From local law enforcement
    • C. Through LAANC or the FAA DroneZone Correct answer
    • D. No authorization is needed if you stay below 400 ft

    Why: Prior authorization for Class B, C, D, or surface Class E airspace comes from the FAA — in practice through LAANC for near-real-time approval, or DroneZone where LAANC is unavailable.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.41

  22. Which of the following operations requires a remote pilot certificate under Part 107?

    • A. Flying for fun in your backyard
    • B. Flying a drone indoors at home
    • C. A recreational park flight after completing TRUST
    • D. Photographing a house for a realtor's listing Correct answer

    Why: Any flight that furthers a business purpose — like real-estate photography — is non-recreational and requires Part 107 certification.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.1

  23. Refusing to submit to a lawful drug or alcohol test can result in:

    • A. Denial of an application, or suspension or revocation of the remote pilot certificate Correct answer
    • B. A written warning only
    • C. A mandatory retest within 30 days
    • D. No consequence under Part 107

    Why: Refusal to submit to testing is grounds for denying a certificate application for up to one year, or suspending/revoking the certificate.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.57, 107.59

  24. Which statement about visual observers (VOs) under Part 107 is true?

    • A. A VO is required for every commercial flight
    • B. VOs must hold their own remote pilot certificate
    • C. Using a VO is optional, and it does not remove the see-and-avoid responsibilities of the RPIC Correct answer
    • D. A VO may watch the aircraft through binoculars full-time

    Why: VOs are optional crew. Even with a VO, the RPIC and person flying must still be able to see the aircraft and maintain awareness; vision aids like binoculars may only be used momentarily.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.31, 107.33

  25. A client wants footage of an antenna tower that stands 700 ft tall. Staying within 400 ft of the structure, how high may you legally fly?

    • A. Up to 1,100 ft AGL Correct answer
    • B. Up to 400 ft AGL, because the Part 107 ceiling has no exception for tall structures
    • C. Up to 700 ft AGL, matching the height of the structure you are inspecting
    • D. Any altitude at all, as long as you remain within a 400-ft radius of the tower

    Why: Within a 400-ft radius of a structure you may fly up to 400 ft above that structure's uppermost limit, so 700 + 400 = 1,100 ft AGL. Step outside the radius and the plain 400-ft ceiling applies again.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.51(b)

  26. You are hired to film a parade, and cars carrying people are moving along the route beneath your planned flight path. Under Part 107:

    • A. flight over moving vehicles is unrestricted as long as you remain above 100 ft AGL
    • B. flight over moving vehicles is always prohibited, and no category of aircraft changes that
    • C. you may overfly the vehicles if every occupant consented to the operation in writing beforehand
    • D. sustained flight over moving vehicles requires an eligible category aircraft Correct answer

    Why: 107.145 permits operations over moving vehicles only with a Category 1-4 eligible aircraft, and outside a closed or restricted site the flight must be a transit rather than sustained loitering overhead.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.145, 107.39

  27. Your drone weighs 0.9 lb and you fly it only for paid real-estate photography. Registration is:

    • A. not required, because registration applies only to aircraft heavier than 55 lb
    • B. required only after you have completed your first ten paid jobs with that aircraft
    • C. required — $5 per aircraft, marked on the exterior Correct answer
    • D. handled automatically by the manufacturer when the aircraft is first activated in its companion app

    Why: Under Part 107 every aircraft must be registered regardless of weight; the 250 g threshold applies only to recreational flying. Registration costs $5 per aircraft, lasts 3 years, and the number must be legible on the exterior.

    Reference: 14 CFR Part 48; 14 CFR 107.13

  28. A treeline will hide the aircraft from you for roughly 20 seconds during a planned route. Your lawful options are to:

    • A. fly the route and rely on the live video feed while the aircraft is behind the trees
    • B. reroute to keep the aircraft in sight, or hold a 107.31 waiver Correct answer
    • C. station a visual observer behind the treeline and continue, since the observer preserves line of sight
    • D. climb above 400 ft AGL for that segment so the aircraft stays visible over the trees

    Why: Visual line of sight must be maintained by the RPIC or the person manipulating the controls, unaided by anything but corrective lenses. A video feed does not satisfy it and a distant observer cannot substitute for it.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.31, 107.33, 107.205

  29. Your recurrent training lapsed six weeks ago, and today a client asks for a paid flight. You may:

    • A. not fly under Part 107 until you complete the recurrent training Correct answer
    • B. fly, because the remote pilot certificate itself never expires
    • C. fly under the supervision of any other certificated remote pilot for up to 90 days
    • D. fly, provided you retake and pass the initial knowledge test at a testing center within 30 days

    Why: The certificate does not expire, but its privileges lapse unless recurrent training was completed within the previous 24 calendar months. The free online course takes under an hour and restores the privileges immediately.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.65

  30. A paid shoot in Class G airspace will run until 45 minutes after official sunset. The flight is legal if:

    • A. the aircraft remains below 200 ft AGL for the entire post-sunset portion
    • B. a visual observer on site also holds a remote pilot certificate
    • C. the operation was filed as a night waiver in DroneZone at least 90 days in advance
    • D. the aircraft has anti-collision lighting visible for 3 statute miles Correct answer

    Why: Night flight is a standard privilege for pilots whose knowledge test or recurrent training covered night operations. The remaining hard requirement is anti-collision lighting visible for 3 SM with an adequate flash rate.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.29

  31. Which Part 107 rule may NOT be waived by the FAA?

    • A. The 400-ft altitude limit
    • B. The visual line of sight requirement
    • C. The restriction on operating from a moving vehicle over other than sparsely populated areas
    • D. The prohibition on carrying hazardous materials Correct answer

    Why: 107.205 lists the sections the FAA may waive, including altitude, visual line of sight, and operations over people. The hazardous materials prohibition in 107.36 is absent from that list and can never be waived.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.205, 107.36

  32. Where does an operator apply for a Part 107 waiver?

    • A. Through the FAA DroneZone waiver portal Correct answer
    • B. By calling the nearest Flight Standards District Office and requesting a verbal approval
    • C. Through whichever LAANC service provider handles airspace authorizations for that area
    • D. By filing a NOTAM at least 90 days before the date of the planned operation

    Why: Waiver requests are submitted electronically through the FAA DroneZone portal. LAANC handles airspace authorizations only; it cannot grant a deviation from an operating rule.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.200

  33. A Part 107 waiver application must demonstrate what?

    • A. That the same operation has already been flown safely at least once under a certificate of authorization
    • B. That no alternative method of complying with the rule exists for the proposed operation
    • C. That the applicant carries liability insurance appropriate to the scale of the operation
    • D. That the operation can be performed safely under the terms of the certificate Correct answer

    Why: The applicant must show the FAA that the proposed operation can be conducted safely under the waiver's terms, usually by describing the mitigations that replace the protection the waived rule provided.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.200

  34. How far ahead of a planned operation does the FAA advise submitting a waiver request?

    • A. At least 90 days Correct answer
    • B. At least 10 business days, which is the maximum processing time the FAA commits to
    • C. At least 24 hours, the same lead time that applies to airspace authorization requests
    • D. At least 30 calendar days, after which the request is approved automatically if the FAA has not replied

    Why: The FAA asks for at least 90 days because complex requests need review and rework. Nothing is ever approved by default; an unanswered request is simply not a waiver.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.200; FAA waiver guidance

  35. You plan a daylight flight at 300 ft AGL inside Class D airspace, within visual line of sight. The operation requires:

    • A. a 107.41 waiver, because any entry into controlled airspace is a deviation from Part 107
    • B. both an airspace authorization and an altitude waiver, since 300 ft exceeds the LAANC ceiling everywhere
    • C. nothing beyond the normal preflight, because Class D reverts to Class G below 400 ft AGL
    • D. an airspace authorization, not a waiver Correct answer

    Why: Entering controlled airspace with prior ATC authorization is compliance with 107.41, not a deviation from it. A waiver is needed only to depart from a waivable operating rule, and none of the limits here are being exceeded.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.41, 107.200

  36. A Certificate of Waiver is issued with special provisions attached. Those provisions:

    • A. may be disregarded whenever the remote pilot in command judges conditions to be safer than anticipated
    • B. are binding, and operating outside them voids the waiver Correct answer
    • C. apply only to the specific aircraft serial number that was listed in the application
    • D. expire 12 calendar months after issue regardless of the dates printed on the certificate

    Why: The provisions are the conditions on which the FAA accepted the risk. Flying outside them means flying without a waiver, so the underlying rule applies again and is being broken.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.200, 107.205

  37. Under subpart D, an operation over human beings may be conducted without a waiver when:

    • A. the people underneath are wearing head protection appropriate to the weight of the aircraft
    • B. the aircraft weighs less than 250 g and the entire flight is conducted below 100 ft AGL
    • C. the aircraft meets an eligible category and its operating requirements Correct answer
    • D. everyone underneath has been briefed on the operation and has given verbal consent

    Why: Categories 1 through 4 replaced the old blanket prohibition. Each category sets its own limits on injury severity, exposed rotating parts, labelling, and airworthiness, and the aircraft must qualify before the operation is legal without a waiver.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.110 through 107.145

  38. How long is a Part 107 Certificate of Waiver valid?

    • A. For the period printed on the certificate Correct answer
    • B. Indefinitely, unless and until the FAA revokes it in writing for cause
    • C. For 24 calendar months, matching the remote pilot recurrent training cycle
    • D. For the duration of the single operation that was described in the waiver application

    Why: Each certificate carries its own effective and expiration dates, commonly up to four years for a standing waiver. Read the certificate rather than assuming a fixed term.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.200

  39. Since the 2021 rule change, conducting a Part 107 operation at night requires:

    • A. a 107.29 waiver, applied for through DroneZone at least 90 days in advance
    • B. a 107.29 waiver, but only when the flight also passes over people or moving vehicles
    • C. no additional action of any kind, because the FAA removed the night restriction from Part 107 entirely
    • D. updated recurrent training plus anti-collision lighting visible for 3 statute miles Correct answer

    Why: The waiver requirement was removed, but two conditions replaced it: training that covers night operations, and anti-collision lighting visible for at least 3 statute miles. Removing the waiver did not remove the rule.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.29, 107.65

  40. Which of the following still requires a waiver rather than a routine authorization or a standard preflight step?

    • A. Flying beyond visual line of sight Correct answer
    • B. Flying at night with anti-collision lighting fitted to the aircraft
    • C. Flying in Class C airspace at an altitude that LAANC has approved
    • D. Climbing to 400 ft above a tower while remaining within 400 ft of it

    Why: Beyond visual line of sight is a deviation from 107.31 and needs a Certificate of Waiver. The other three are ordinary compliant operations, one of which needs an airspace authorization rather than a waiver.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.31, 107.51, 107.200

  41. Section 107.23 prohibits operating a small unmanned aircraft in a careless or reckless manner. This section:

    • A. applies only when the operation results in an accident that has to be reported to the FAA
    • B. may be waived for closed-set filming when a site safety plan accompanies the application
    • C. applies only to operations flown for compensation or hire rather than recreationally
    • D. is a catch-all the FAA can cite even when no other rule was broken Correct answer

    Why: Careless or reckless operation stands on its own. An operation can comply with every numeric limit in Part 107 and still be cited under 107.23, and the section is not on the list of rules the FAA may waive.

    Reference: 14 CFR 107.23, 107.205

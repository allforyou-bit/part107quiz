What the real Part 107 exam actually looks like

The Unmanned Aircraft General — Small (UAG) knowledge test is 60 multiple-choice questions in 120 minutes, taken in person at a PSI testing center. There is no penalty for guessing, the pass mark is 70% — 42 correct out of 60 — and you get your result before you leave the building. This simulation mirrors that structure: 60 scored questions, the 120-minute limit, the 70% pass mark and the FAA's current area weighting.

The five knowledge areas and what each is worth

The FAA's testing vendor, PSI, publishes how heavily each area is weighted (UAG Applicant Information Bulletin, effective September 29, 2025), and the FAA points candidates to that bulletin in place of the older blueprint printed in the Remote Pilot ACS. They are also the single most useful thing to know before you decide where to spend your study time.

Operations is a quarter of the exam, and it is not common sense

Read the table again. Operations is a quarter of your scored questions (25%), second only to Regulations at 48%. It is also easy to under-study, because it looks like common sense: aeronautical decision making, the five hazardous attitudes and their antidotes, crew resource management, fitness for flight, radio phraseology, and emergency procedures. That resemblance to common sense is the trap. These questions are written so that two options both sound sensible and only one matches the FAA's published doctrine. Answer as the handbook would, not as you would.

Why our drills give four choices when the real test gives three

The live FAA exam presents three answer choices per question. Ours present four. That is deliberate: a fourth plausible distractor cuts the odds of a blind guess from one in three to one in four, so your practice score reflects what you actually know rather than what you got away with. If you are consistently clearing 85% here, the real paper's three-choice format works in your favour, not against you.

How to use this simulation

Run it cold the first time — timer on, notes away, no pausing. The score matters less than the per-area breakdown you get at the end, because that breakdown is your study plan. Whatever area comes back weakest, read its study guide, work through that topic's full written question set, then draw a fresh 60.

Timing is rarely the constraint. Two hours for 60 scored questions is two minutes each, but PSI also adds 5 unscored validation questions inside that time, so aim to leave some minutes over. Use the surplus: flag anything you were unsure of, finish the paper, then go back to the flags. Changing an answer on review is not the mistake folklore claims it is — changing it without a reason is.

A practical readiness bar is two consecutive mocks at 85% or better. That leaves a 15-point cushion over the 70% pass mark, which is about the margin you want for test-day nerves and for the handful of questions phrased in a way you have not seen before.

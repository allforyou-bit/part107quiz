Pick an answer, then open “Show the answer”. These 25 questions are original practice questions written from public federal sources; they are not taken from any certification test.

Environmental impacts

  1. A homeowner asks about the environmental impact of the HFC refrigerant in a new system. Which statement matches EPA's description of HFCs?

    • A. They do not deplete ozone and have no effect on climate
    • B. They deplete ozone slightly but are not greenhouse gases
    • C. They deplete ozone and are also potent greenhouse gases
    • D. They do not deplete ozone but are potent greenhouse gases
    Show the answer

    Answer: They do not deplete ozone but are potent greenhouse gases

    Why: EPA's technician FAQ says that while HFCs are not ozone-depleting substances, they are potent greenhouse gases that contribute to climate change, and EPA's Test Topics glossary says all HFCs have an ODP of 0. Options that say HFCs deplete ozone conflict with that ODP, and the option denying any climate effect conflicts with EPA's greenhouse-gas statement.

    Topic C-ENV · Reference: EPA web page "Technicians and Contractors: Frequent Questions" (updated 2026-03-10), Q. Are there any restrictions on the purchase of hydrofluorocarbon (HFC) refrigerants?; EPA web page "Test Topics" (updated 2025-12-05), glossary: hydrofluorocarbon

  2. When the stratospheric ozone layer is depleted, which part of the sun's radiation reaching the Earth's surface increases most?

    • A. UV-A radiation
    • B. UV-B radiation
    • C. UV-C radiation
    • D. Visible light
    Show the answer

    Answer: UV-B radiation

    Why: EPA's page on the health and environmental effects of ozone layer depletion says depletion of the ozone layer increases primarily the amount of UV-B radiation that reaches the surface. UV-A and visible light are only weakly absorbed by the ozone layer to begin with, and EPA's glossary says UV-C is completely absorbed by ozone and normal oxygen.

    Topic C-ENV · Reference: EPA web page "Health and Environmental Effects of Ozone Layer Depletion" (updated 2026-04-09), Figure Q2-1 caption; EPA web page "Basic Ozone Layer Science" (updated 2026-03-04), glossary: ultraviolet (UV) light

  3. According to EPA, where is most of the Earth's atmospheric ozone concentrated?

    • A. In the stratosphere, about 9 to 18 miles above the surface
    • B. In the troposphere, about 9 to 18 miles above the surface
    • C. In the troposphere, within about 6 miles of the surface
    • D. In the stratosphere, within about 6 miles of the surface
    Show the answer

    Answer: In the stratosphere, about 9 to 18 miles above the surface

    Why: EPA's Basic Ozone Layer Science page says most atmospheric ozone is concentrated in a layer in the stratosphere, about 9 to 18 miles (15 to 30 km) above the Earth's surface. The troposphere is the lowest layer, reaching only about 6 miles up, and the stratosphere runs from about 6 to 31 miles, so neither layer holds most ozone within 6 miles of the surface, and the troposphere does not reach 9 to 18 miles.

    Topic C-ENV · Reference: EPA web page "Basic Ozone Layer Science" (updated 2026-03-04), Section I, The Ozone Layer

  4. A customer asks which refrigerant families carry chlorine, the element that destroys stratospheric ozone. Which families contain it?

    • A. HCFCs and HFCs
    • B. CFCs and HFCs
    • C. CFCs and HCFCs
    • D. CFCs, HCFCs and HFCs
    Show the answer

    Answer: CFCs and HCFCs

    Why: EPA's Test Topics page lists the presence of chlorine in CFC and HCFC refrigerants, and its glossary defines a CFC as chlorine, fluorine and carbon, an HCFC as hydrogen, chlorine, fluorine and carbon, and an HFC as hydrogen, fluorine and carbon. HFCs therefore carry no chlorine, so any answer that includes HFCs or leaves out HCFCs is wrong.

    Topic C-ENV · Reference: EPA web page "Test Topics" (updated 2025-12-05), Core, Environmental Impacts / glossary: chlorofluorocarbon / glossary: hydrochlorofluorocarbon / glossary: hydrofluorocarbon

Clean Air Act and Montreal Protocol

  1. According to EPA's class I phaseout page, when did the ban on production and import of CFCs take effect in the United States?

    • A. January 1, 1994
    • B. January 1, 1995
    • C. January 1, 1996
    • D. January 1, 2010
    Show the answer

    Answer: January 1, 1996

    Why: EPA's class I phaseout page says the ban on production and import of halons took effect on January 1, 1994, and the ban on production and import of other class I ODS, excluding methyl bromide, took effect on January 1, 1996; its schedule shows CFCs at a 75 percent reduction in 1995. EPA's technician FAQ says HCFC-22 production and import have been restricted since 2010.

    Topic C-LAW · Reference: EPA web page "Phaseout of Class I Ozone-Depleting Substances" (updated 2026-03-10), introduction / Table 1: Phaseout Schedule for Class I Substances; EPA web page "Technicians and Contractors: Frequent Questions" (updated 2026-03-10), Q. When will HCFC-22 (or blends that contain HCFC-22 and/or HCFC-142b) no longer be available for purchase?

  2. Under 40 CFR 82.154(a)(2), a small release of R-22 while removing refrigerant counts as de minimis only when certain conditions are met. Which of these is one of those conditions?

    • A. Certified recovery or recycling machines are used
    • B. The amount released is less than one pound
    • C. A certified technician reports the release to EPA
    • D. The appliance holds 5 pounds or less of refrigerant
    Show the answer

    Answer: Certified recovery or recycling machines are used

    Why: 40 CFR 82.154(a)(2)(i) says releases are de minimis only if they occur when the practices in 82.155 and 82.156 (and 82.157 for appliances with ozone-depleting refrigerant) are observed, recovery and/or recycling machines that meet 82.158 are used whenever refrigerant is removed, the technician certification provisions in 82.161 are observed, and the reclamation requirements in 82.164 are observed; 40 CFR 82.154(b)(2) calls this equipment certified under 82.158. The paragraph sets no pound limit, no report to EPA and no charge-size condition.

    Topic C-LAW · Reference: 40 CFR 82.154(a)(2); 40 CFR 82.154(a)(2)(i); 40 CFR 82.154(b)(2)

  3. A facility maintenance worker who holds no technician certification opens a valve and lets refrigerant escape from an R-123 chiller while working on it. Is that worker subject to the venting prohibition in 40 CFR 82.154(a)(1)?

    • A. Yes, but only if the chiller holds 50 or more pounds
    • B. No; it covers only technicians certified under 82.161
    • C. No; it covers only the appliance's owner or operator
    • D. Yes; any person servicing an appliance is covered
    Show the answer

    Answer: Yes; any person servicing an appliance is covered

    Why: 40 CFR 82.154(a)(1) says no person maintaining, servicing, repairing, or disposing of an appliance may knowingly vent or otherwise release any refrigerant from it, and 40 CFR 82.152 defines a person as any individual or legal entity. R-123 is a class II refrigerant, not one of the substitutes 82.154(a)(1) exempts, and the prohibition does not depend on certification, charge size or ownership.

    Topic C-LAW · Reference: 40 CFR 82.154(a)(1); 40 CFR 82.152, "Person"; EPA web page "Ozone-Depleting Substances" (updated 2026-04-09), Class II ODS table

  4. Which substitute refrigerant does 40 CFR 82.154(a)(1) exempt from the venting prohibition in any application, not just in listed end-uses?

    • A. Ammonia
    • B. Carbon dioxide
    • C. Propane (R-290)
    • D. R-1234yf
    Show the answer

    Answer: Carbon dioxide

    Why: 40 CFR 82.154(a)(1) exempts carbon dioxide, nitrogen and water in any application. Ammonia is exempt only in commercial or industrial process refrigeration or in absorption units, propane only in the end-uses listed in 82.154(a)(1)(viii), and EPA's 2024 Federal Register preamble lists HFO-1234yf (R-1234yf) among the refrigerants to which none of the exemptions apply.

    Topic C-LAW · Reference: 40 CFR 82.154(a)(1)(i)-(iv); 89 FR 50418 (June 13, 2024), section II.A

Section 608 regulations

  1. Assume each container below has the fitting made for its intended use. Under 40 CFR 82.154(c), which sale to a buyer with no technician certification is allowed?

    • A. A 1-pound self-sealing can of R-134a for a car
    • B. A 25-pound cylinder of R-134a for the buyer's car
    • C. A 1-pound self-sealing can of R-22 for a home unit
    • D. A 30-pound cylinder of R-410A for a home unit
    Show the answer

    Answer: A 1-pound self-sealing can of R-134a for a car

    Why: 40 CFR 82.154(c)(1)(ix) excepts a non-exempt substitute refrigerant intended for use in an MVAC and sold in a container designed to hold two pounds or less, with a unique fitting and (if made or imported on or after January 1, 2018) a self-sealing valve; EPA's sales restriction page gives R-134a for motor vehicle air conditioners as the example. A 25-pound cylinder is too large for that exception even for a car, R-22 is an HCFC rather than a substitute, so the small-can exception does not reach it, and a 30-pound cylinder of R-410A for a home system fits no exception.

    Topic C-REG · Reference: 40 CFR 82.154(c)(1)(ix); EPA web page "Refrigerant Sales Restriction" (updated 2026-03-23), Overview / Requirements to Purchase Refrigerant; EPA web page "Test Topics" (updated 2025-12-05), Core, Environmental Impacts

  2. A company plans to import a new model of refrigerant recovery machine for sale in the United States. Under 40 CFR 82.158(d), who must certify that model?

    • A. EPA, by reviewing the importer's own test data
    • B. An approved technician certification program
    • C. The importer, by a signed self-declaration
    • D. An approved equipment testing organization
    Show the answer

    Answer: An approved equipment testing organization

    Why: 40 CFR 82.158(d) requires manufacturers and importers of recovery and/or recycling equipment to have it certified by an approved equipment testing organization, and 40 CFR 82.160 has testing organizations apply to EPA for approval and bars them from certifying equipment before that approval; EPA's Test Topics page calls this the need for third-party certification. EPA approves the testing organizations rather than certifying models from an importer's own data, technician certification programs certify people rather than machines, and the rule has no self-certification by importers.

    Topic C-REG · Reference: 40 CFR 82.158(d); 40 CFR 82.160(a); 40 CFR 82.160(c); EPA web page "Test Topics" (updated 2025-12-05), Core, Section 608 Regulations

  3. A certified technician shows a certification card to buy a cylinder of R-410A for a neighbor, who plans to charge it into the neighbor's own system. According to EPA's Q&A for Section 608 certified technicians, this purchase is:

    • A. Allowed, as the card holder is the buyer
    • B. A violation, as the recipient holds no card
    • C. Allowed if the neighbor's system holds under 5 pounds
    • D. A violation only if the neighbor pays the card holder
    Show the answer

    Answer: A violation, as the recipient holds no card

    Why: EPA's Q&A answers no to using a certification card to buy refrigerant for a person who is not certified: it is a violation to sell, distribute, or offer for sale or distribution a refrigerant for use in an appliance unless the recipient is, or employs, a certified technician. Standing at the counter as the card holder does not change who receives the refrigerant, there is no 5-pound exception, and payment does not matter, since distribution is covered too.

    Topic C-REG · Reference: EPA web page "Questions and Answers for Section 608 Certified Technicians" (updated 2026-02-24), General Questions

  4. Under 40 CFR 82.158(d), recovery and/or recycling equipment certified for appliances other than small appliances, MVACs and MVAC-like appliances must have which item on all of its hoses?

    • A. Self-sealing valves
    • B. Noncondensable purge devices
    • C. Low-loss fittings
    • D. Pressure relief valves
    Show the answer

    Answer: Low-loss fittings

    Why: 40 CFR 82.158(d)(7) says the equipment must be equipped with low-loss fittings on all hoses, and 40 CFR 82.152 defines a low-loss fitting as a connection device designed to close automatically or to be closed manually when disconnected, minimizing the release of refrigerant. Self-sealing valves are the 82.154(c)(2) requirement for small cans of MVAC refrigerant, a noncondensables purge device is optional equipment that 82.158(d)(6) limits if present, and the rule puts no relief valves on hoses.

    Topic C-REG · Reference: 40 CFR 82.158(d)(7); 40 CFR 82.152, "Low-loss fitting"; 40 CFR 82.154(c)(2)(i); 40 CFR 82.158(d)(6)

  5. A certified technician recovers the R-134a from a vending machine as 40 CFR 82.156(b) requires. An uncertified helper who is not an apprentice then does the remaining steps. Under the 40 CFR 82.152 definition of technician, which step still has to be done by a certified technician?

    • A. Cutting out the old filter-drier
    • B. Recharging the repaired system
    • C. Shipping the recovered refrigerant
    • D. Replacing the condenser fan motor
    Show the answer

    Answer: Recharging the repaired system

    Why: The 40 CFR 82.152 definition of technician says activities on appliances properly evacuated under 82.156 are not reasonably expected to release refrigerant unless the activity includes adding refrigerant, so recharging still needs a certified technician. Cutting out a filter-drier on a properly evacuated unit falls under that rule and is not expected to release refrigerant. Shipping recovered refrigerant does not touch the appliance's refrigerant circuit, and the 82.152 definition turns on violating the integrity of that circuit. Replacing a condenser fan motor is electrical work of the kind 82.152 lists as not violating the integrity of the refrigerant circuit.

    Topic C-REG · Reference: 40 CFR 82.152, "Technician"

  6. Assume a refrigerant has a liquid-phase saturation pressure of 300 psia at 104°F (a value given for this question only). Under 40 CFR 82.152, an appliance that uses this refrigerant is which kind of appliance?

    • A. A medium-pressure appliance
    • B. A very high-pressure appliance
    • C. A low-pressure appliance
    • D. A high-pressure appliance
    Show the answer

    Answer: A high-pressure appliance

    Why: 40 CFR 82.152 defines a high-pressure appliance as one using a refrigerant with a liquid-phase saturation pressure between 170 psia and 355 psia at 104°F, and 300 psia falls inside that band. A medium-pressure appliance is defined by 45 to 170 psia, below this value. A very high-pressure appliance needs more than 355 psia at 104°F (or a critical temperature below 104°F). A low-pressure appliance is defined by less than 45 psia.

    Topic C-REG · Reference: 40 CFR 82.152, "High-pressure appliance"; 40 CFR 82.152, "Medium-pressure appliance"; 40 CFR 82.152, "Very high-pressure appliance"; 40 CFR 82.152, "Low-pressure appliance"

  7. A certified technician retires from the trade on December 31, 2026. Under 40 CFR 82.161(a)(4), where must a copy of the certificate be kept, and until when?

    • A. At the place of business, until December 31, 2029
    • B. On the technician's person, until December 31, 2029
    • C. At the place of business, until December 31, 2026
    • D. On the technician's person, until December 31, 2026
    Show the answer

    Answer: At the place of business, until December 31, 2029

    Why: 40 CFR 82.161(a)(4) requires certified technicians to keep a copy of their certificate at their place of business and to maintain a copy until three years after no longer operating as a technician; three years after December 31, 2026 is December 31, 2029. EPA's Q&A adds that EPA does not require technicians to carry the card while servicing appliances. Keeping it only until retirement leaves out the three-year period.

    Topic C-REG · Reference: 40 CFR 82.161(a)(4)(i); 40 CFR 82.161(a)(4)(ii); EPA web page "Questions and Answers for Section 608 Certified Technicians" (updated 2026-02-24), General Questions

Substitute refrigerants and oils

  1. A technician is converting an older CFC-12 system to an HFC substitute. Which concern about the system's existing lubricant does EPA's Test Topics page raise?

    • A. It may be incompatible with the replacement refrigerant
    • B. It may make the replacement refrigerant fractionate
    • C. It may break down into decomposition products
    • D. It may hold air and moisture that spoil the new charge
    Show the answer

    Answer: It may be incompatible with the replacement refrigerant

    Why: EPA's Test Topics page flags the incompatibility of substitute refrigerants with many lubricants used with CFC and HCFC refrigerants, and 40 CFR 82.152 says a retrofit may include changes in lubricants to achieve system compatibility with the new refrigerant. Fractionation and decomposition are separate Test Topics items about refrigerants, not concerns the page raises about lubricants. Air and moisture are removed by evacuation at the end of service, another separate Test Topics item that the page does not tie to the old lubricant.

    Topic C-SUB · Reference: EPA web page "Test Topics" (updated 2025-12-05), Core, Substitute Refrigerants and oils / Core, Dehydration Evacuation; 40 CFR 82.152, "Retrofit"

  2. As part of its SNAP review of a refrigerant blend that contains a flammable component, EPA requires fractionation testing. What is that testing meant to ensure?

    • A. That a flammable blend meets reclaimed-refrigerant purity limits
    • B. That the blend's ozone depletion potential stays at zero during use
    • C. That the blend's composition does not change and become flammable
    • D. That the blend can be recovered with equipment not rated for flammable use
    Show the answer

    Answer: That the blend's composition does not change and become flammable

    Why: EPA's Ten Questions page says that if a blend contains a flammable component, EPA requires fractionation testing to ensure that the composition of the blend does not change and become flammable, and 40 CFR 82.178(a)(9) asks for fractionation data on changes in a blend's composition during various leak scenarios. Purity limits for reclaimed refrigerant come from Appendix A to subpart F, not from this test. Ozone depletion potential is evaluated as its own factor. Fractionation testing concerns the blend's composition during leaks, not which recovery equipment may be used.

    Topic C-SUB · Reference: EPA web page "Ten Questions to Ask Before You Purchase An Alternative Refrigerant" (updated 2026-08-31), Is the alternative refrigerant flammable? / Is the refrigerant on the EPA's SNAP Program list of acceptable substitutes; 40 CFR 82.178(a)(9)

Recover, recycle, reclaim

  1. Before changing the compressor oil in an R-404A walk-in cooler, a technician must first lower the system's pressure. What reason does EPA give for this step?

    • A. Lower pressure keeps air and moisture out of the oil
    • B. Oil drained under pressure is a hazardous waste
    • C. The oil can hold large amounts of dissolved refrigerant
    • D. Fresh oil cannot be added while the system is pressurized
    Show the answer

    Answer: The oil can hold large amounts of dissolved refrigerant

    Why: EPA's service practice page says the oil in a refrigeration appliance can contain large amounts of dissolved refrigerant, so EPA requires a pressure reduction before an oil change to ensure the bulk of that refrigerant is recovered. Keeping air and moisture out is the job of evacuation at the end of service. The page refers readers to the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act only for oil disposal, not as the reason for the pressure step. EPA gives no rule about adding fresh oil under pressure as the reason.

    Topic C-3R · Reference: EPA web page "Stationary Refrigeration Service Practice Requirements" (updated 2026-07-06), Changing Refrigerant Oil; EPA web page "Test Topics" (updated 2025-12-05), Core, Dehydration Evacuation

Refrigeration basics and dehydration

  1. According to EPA's 2023 chiller rule, how does a typical chiller deliver cooling to a building's occupied spaces?

    • A. Its refrigerant cools the room air directly at an indoor coil
    • B. It cools water or brine that is pumped to air handlers
    • C. It cools air at a cooling tower that is ducted to the rooms
    • D. It evaporates water into the supply air to cool it
    Show the answer

    Answer: It cools water or brine that is pumped to air handlers

    Why: The 2023 Federal Register preamble (88 FR 26386) describes a chiller as equipment that typically cools water or a brine solution, which is pumped to fan coil units or other air handlers to cool the air supplied to occupied spaces. EPA's SNAP substitutes page says residential and light commercial air conditioners are distinguished from chillers because they condition the air directly. The same preamble names a cooling tower as one place the heat absorbed by the water can be sent, not a source of cooled supply air. Evaporating water to cool is evaporative cooling, an alternative technology EPA names separately.

    Topic C-REF · Reference: 88 FR 26386 (April 28, 2023), section II.A.1; EPA web page "Substitutes in Refrigeration and Air Conditioning" (updated 2026-03-16), Residential and Light Commercial Air Conditioning and Heat Pumps; EPA web page "Questions and Answers About SNAP" (updated 2026-08-31), Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, question 10

  2. According to Appendix A to 40 CFR part 82, subpart F, what are noncondensable gases in refrigerant mainly made of, and what may their presence reflect?

    • A. Mainly air; excess compressor oil carried over into the refrigerant
    • B. Mainly water vapor; poor quality control when refrigerant is moved into cylinders
    • C. Mainly water vapor; excess compressor oil carried over into the refrigerant
    • D. Mainly air; poor quality control when refrigerant is moved into cylinders
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mainly air; poor quality control when refrigerant is moved into cylinders

    Why: Appendix A to subpart F says non-condensable gases consist primarily of air accumulated in the vapor phase of refrigerants, and that their presence may reflect poor quality control in transferring refrigerants to storage tanks and cylinders. Oil carryover is characterized separately as high boiling residue, so it is not the cause named. Water is a separate contaminant classification from air and other non-condensables. Pairing water vapor with oil carryover combines both errors.

    Topic C-REF · Reference: 40 CFR part 82, subpart F, Appendix A, section 5.10.1; 40 CFR part 82, subpart F, Appendix A, sections 4.1.2-4.1.5

A2L and A3 refrigerants

  1. A technician's recovery machine, made in 2019 for appliances other than small appliances, is certified under Appendix B3 of 40 CFR part 82, subpart F. Under 40 CFR 82.158(d)(3), which refrigerants does that certification cover?

    • A. Flammable refrigerants only
    • B. Both flammable and non-flammable refrigerants
    • C. Non-flammable refrigerants only
    • D. Non-flammable and mildly flammable (2L) refrigerants
    Show the answer

    Answer: Non-flammable refrigerants only

    Why: 40 CFR 82.158(d)(3) requires recovery equipment manufactured or imported on or after January 1, 2017, for appliances other than small appliances, MVACs and MVAC-like appliances, to be certified under appendix B3 for non-flammable refrigerants or appendix B4 for flammable refrigerants, and 40 CFR 82.154(b)(2) requires recovery equipment certified for that type of refrigerant and appliance. A B3 certification therefore does not cover flammable refrigerants, alone or together with non-flammable ones, and that includes mildly flammable 2L refrigerants: EPA's 2015 Federal Register preamble calls class 2L mildly flammable, its 2024 preamble says 2L refrigerants such as R-454B are flammable, and subpart G's A2L listings say any recovery equipment used should be designed for flammable refrigerants.

    Topic C-A2L · Reference: 40 CFR 82.158(d)(3); 40 CFR part 82, subpart F, Appendix B4 (title); 40 CFR 82.154(b)(2); 80 FR 19462 (April 10, 2015), section III.B; 89 FR 50428 (June 13, 2024), section II.C; 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, Appendix Y, listing 7 (cold storage warehouses), further information

  2. While replacing a section of piping at a service port on a new R-454A cold storage warehouse system, a technician removes the red marking. What do the subpart G, Appendix Y use conditions require?

    • A. Restore it to at least 1 inch (25 mm) in both directions
    • B. Restore it to at least 9/16 inch (15 mm) in both directions
    • C. Restore it to at least 1 inch (25 mm) from the compressor
    • D. Restore it to at least 9/16 inch (15 mm) from the compressor
    Show the answer

    Answer: Restore it to at least 1 inch (25 mm) in both directions

    Why: 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, Appendix Y requires the red marking at all service ports and other places where the refrigerant circuit may be opened; it must extend a minimum of 1 inch (25 mm) in both directions from such locations and shall be replaced if removed. Nine-sixteenths of an inch (15 mm) is the minimum height of the GHS flame-symbol diamond in the same listing, and measuring 1 inch from the compressor is the Appendix R wording for hydrocarbon vending machines, not the Appendix Y rule.

    Topic C-A2L · Reference: 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, Appendix Y, listing 7 (cold storage warehouses); 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, Appendix Y, listing 7 (cold storage warehouses), use conditions; 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, Appendix R, listing 6 (vending machines), use conditions

  3. A restaurant owner asks a technician to convert an existing commercial ice machine, designed for another refrigerant, to R-1234yf. Under the use conditions in 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, Appendix Y, may the technician do this?

    • A. Yes, if red markings and warning labels are added
    • B. No; R-1234yf is listed only for vehicle air conditioners
    • C. Yes, if flammable-rated recovery equipment is used
    • D. No; the listing covers only new equipment built for it
    Show the answer

    Answer: No; the listing covers only new equipment built for it

    Why: 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, Appendix Y lists R-1234yf (HFO-1234yf) for commercial ice machines only in new equipment specifically designed and clearly identified for the refrigerant; none of these substitutes may be used as a conversion or retrofit refrigerant for existing equipment designed for other refrigerants. R-1234yf is not limited to vehicle air conditioners, since the same appendix lists it for new commercial ice machines, and red markings, warning labels or recovery equipment designed for flammable refrigerants are separate conditions or recommendations that do not make an existing machine eligible.

    Topic C-A2L · Reference: 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, Appendix Y, listing 4 (commercial ice machines); 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, Appendix Y, listing 4 (commercial ice machines), use conditions

Safety

  1. Subpart G tells technicians to wear protective gloves and to take special care to avoid skin contact when handling R-290. What hazard does it give as the reason?

    • A. Heat burns from the hot discharge piping
    • B. Freeze burns to skin the refrigerant touches
    • C. Toxic absorption of vapor through the skin
    • D. Static sparks from bare skin igniting vapor
    Show the answer

    Answer: Freeze burns to skin the refrigerant touches

    Why: 40 CFR part 82, subpart G (Appendix R, vending machines listing) says special care should be taken to avoid contact with the skin because these refrigerants, like many refrigerants, can cause freeze burns on the skin, and that technicians should wear chemical goggles and protective gloves. Burns from hot piping, toxic absorption through the skin and static sparks are not the reason the appendix gives for avoiding skin contact.

    Topic C-SAF · Reference: 40 CFR part 82, subpart G, Appendix R, listing 6 (vending machines), further information

    • B. Division 2.1
    • C. Division 2.3

    Show the answer

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the EPA 608 test?

Each section has 25 questions. Core plus one Type is 50 questions; Universal is 100: 25 Core and 25 from each of Type I, Type II and Type III (40 CFR part 82, subpart F, Appendix D).

What score do I need to pass?

70 percent on the closed-book Type I, Type II, Type III and Universal tests: 18 of 25 in a section. A mail-in Type I test needs 84 percent (Appendix D (e)).

Is the EPA 608 test open book?

Type II, Type III and Universal tests are closed-book and proctored. Programs that offer Type I only may use an on-site or a mail-in format, but a mail-in Type I test cannot be used toward Universal (Appendix D (a), (b)).

Which certification do I need?

Type I for small appliances; Type II for medium-, high- and very high-pressure appliances other than small appliances, MVACs and MVAC-like appliances; Type III for low-pressure appliances; Universal for all three (40 CFR 82.161(a)(1)). Servicing motor vehicle air conditioners for consideration is covered by Section 609 (40 CFR part 82, subpart B), not Section 608.

Does EPA 608 certification expire?

No. EPA’s Section 608 Technician Certification Requirements page says Section 608 technician certification credentials do not expire.

Are these questions from the certification test?

No. They are original practice questions written from public federal sources. The questions for the certification test come from EPA, which releases them only to approved technician certification programs (40 CFR 82.161(c)). EPA does not review or approve any training preparatory programs or materials.

What changed for 2027?

Certifying organizations report that EPA updated the test bank in August 2026 and that exams from the previous bank end on January 1, 2027. The practice questions here are written from the current regulations and EPA pages; they do not claim to match the content or question count of the exams given from 2027.
